SPHERE Judgment Simulator

Can blood tell us about judgment? Try it yourself.

My findings, in very simple words

  • My question: Can 3 things in blood tell us how good a person's judgment is?
  • My data: 610 fake people, made to look like real data. Not real patients.
  • What I found: When p-tau217 goes up, judgment gets worse, and overall thinking goes down.
  • What mattered most: p-tau217. It did about half the work. Then NFL, then GFAP.
  • How good was it? My model explained about 1 in 3 parts of judgment, 34.3%. The other 2 parts are still a mystery.
  • A surprise: The simple model did better than the fancy model when I tested it 5 times. The fancy model learned the practice test too well, and did worse on new data.
  • What this does NOT mean: It does not prove blood causes bad judgment. It does not diagnose anyone. It does not predict real money choices.
Learning only. Not medical advice. Not a diagnosis.
This uses synthetic Stanford ADRC SPHERE data, 610 people, not real patients. It does not tell you anything about a real person. Do not use it to make health or money decisions.

1. Pick a blood profile

Typical values in the data: p-tau217 median 0.28, GFAP median 72, NFL median 25. Low = 25th percentile, Medium = median, High = 75th percentile.

2. What the model predicts

Predicted judgment score, b4_judgment: 0.37

About average. Higher means worse judgment.

Predicted MoCA total, c2_mocatots: 23.2

About average for this synthetic data, mean 23.2.

Formulas, from the full 610 people, linear regression:
Judgment = -0.136 + 0.640 x p-tau217 + 0.002 x GFAP + 0.007 x NFL
MoCA total = 27.809 - 7.102 x p-tau217 - 0.014 x GFAP - 0.042 x NFL

3. Try changing one thing

Keep GFAP and NFL at medium, and move p-tau217 from low to high. Watch judgment go up and MoCA go down. That is the main pattern in the data: higher p-tau217, worse judgment and lower overall cognition. P-tau217 mattered most in the Random Forest too, 51.7% of the importance.

R2 is variance explained. 0.343 means 34.3%. Random Forest train R2 was about 0.89, test R2 only about 0.34, so it overfits. The linear model is simpler and more stable, which is why this simulator uses it.

5. What this does NOT do

  • It does not use real patient data. All IDs are Synthetic, like Synthetic537.
  • It does not prove p-tau217 causes bad judgment. Correlation is not cause.
  • It does not diagnose Alzheimer’s or any disease.
  • It does not predict real money decisions, only test scores in synthetic data.

Data: Stanford ADRC SPHERE, synthetic cohort, 2026 release. Biomarkers 618 people, cognitive scores 634 people, joined 610 people. Code and full results: GitHub git-gunveer/Stanford-SPHERE-Neuroeconomics, and the 1-page summary in my research portfolio.
