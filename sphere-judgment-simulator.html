New here? Start here, in 60 seconds

1
Your brain makes money choices. Judgment is how well you think through a choice, solve a problem, and avoid a bad decision.
2
Blood carries clues. Three proteins in blood go up or down when the brain is under stress or changing. They are called p-tau217, GFAP, and NFL. Think of them like warning lights on a car dashboard.
3
My question. If I only know those 3 blood numbers, can I guess a person's judgment score?
4
My answer, in short. Partly, yes. About 1 in 3 parts. The other 2 parts are still a mystery. And p-tau217 matters most.
p-tau217
The star player. A protein linked to Alzheimer's changes. Higher usually means more brain change. It did about half the work in my model.
GFAP
A stress signal from brain support cells. Think of it as the brain's repair crew working overtime.
NFL
A signal that brain wires are getting damaged. Think of it as frayed cables.

My findings, in very simple words

  • When p-tau217 goes up, judgment gets worse. Correlation +0.537. That is the strongest link I found.
  • When p-tau217 goes up, overall thinking goes down. MoCA correlation -0.541.
  • p-tau217 mattered most. In the first Random Forest, 51.7%. Then NFL 27.5%, then GFAP 20.9%.
  • How good was it? About 1 in 3 parts explained. Tuned model: judgment test 0.375, MoCA test 0.381. That means 37.5% and 38.1%.
  • The fix that made it better: My first fancy model memorized the practice data, train 0.89, test 0.34. I made it smaller, depth 3, at least 5 people per leaf, 200 trees. Now train 0.40, test 0.38. Stable.
  • The simple model was just as good. Linear regression: judgment cross-validation 0.304, MoCA 0.279. Same as the tuned Random Forest. Simple can win.

What is judgment? What is MoCA? A quick glossary

Judgment, b4_judgment
A doctor's score for how well a person makes decisions and solves problems. Higher = worse. Average 0.37.
MoCA total
A short thinking test, out of 30 in real life. Here it is a score in the data. Higher = better thinking. Average 23.2.
Serial 7s
Count backwards by 7s: 100, 93, 86... It tests math and focus. My model was worst here, only 0.17 in cross-validation.
R2, variance explained
If R2 is 0.38, the model explains 38% of the differences between people. The rest is other stuff I did not measure.
Train vs test
Train = practice questions the model saw. Test = new questions it never saw. If train is much higher than test, the model memorized, it did not learn.
5-fold cross-validation
Split the data into 5 parts, test on each part in turn, average the score. Harder to get lucky.

What this does NOT do

  • It does not use real patient data. All IDs are Synthetic, like Synthetic537.
  • It does not prove p-tau217 causes bad judgment. Two things moving together is not proof one causes the other.
  • It does not diagnose Alzheimer's or any disease.
  • It does not predict real money decisions, only test scores in synthetic data.
  • It does not work for one person alone. It is about patterns across 610 people.
